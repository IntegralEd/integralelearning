An Organization's Introduction to eLearning and Platform Selection

Do you need an LMS? Maybe one day, but you probably don't need to decide now.

If you're on the fence, odds are good you're not a school system or IHE, and you may not need one at all.

↓
01 · Common myths

Starting around 2020, when pandemic closures sent many professional learning organizations into asynchronous delivery, platforms built for schools felt necessary for any nonprofit trying to reach external audiences or internal staff. It turns out those platforms are often:

  • Expensive: per-seat pricing adds up quickly and makes "cost of ownership" a tricky variable.
  • Not a great fit: enterprise LMS focus on capturing grades and credit requirements while integrating with a district or university's student information. Nonprofits rarely need or care.
  • Compliance driven: built around requirements for learners, not flexibly engaging professionals or volunteers.

Our team has supported over a dozen nonprofits delivering professional development internally and externally, and we've learned to ask a few questions we'll share with you below. When you're ready, we offer a platform selection service. But we think it's best to start by delivering some learning modules to your intended audience.

03 · The trap

The lock-in trap

Don't lock into a platform until you know how your audience will use it. Platforms often charge per learner, bind you to multi-year licenses, and require costly integration with your other systems. A pilot answers the questions a contract can't:

What's the market? Desktop or mobile? Who's the customer? When & how do they use it?

Skip the lock-in: get learning assets in front of your audience as a trial, test, or pilot, and learn.

05 · Self-assessment

So, do you need an LMS?

Two honest questions. Thirty seconds.

QUESTION 1 OF 2
Do you have a hard requirement today for tracking completions, certifications, or compliance reporting?
QUESTION 2 OF 2
Do you already know your audience: their devices, their habits, and how many of them there are?
YOU'RE READY
Shop for an LMS, carefully.
Negotiate short initial terms, confirm SCORM import and export, and keep every asset in universal formats so the platform serves you, not the other way around.
Start over
GOOD NEWS: YOU CAN WAIT
Pilot first. Decide later.
Launch a few learning modules in universal formats to gauge business requirements, adoption, and user behavior. Let real usage data, not a sales demo, pick your platform.
